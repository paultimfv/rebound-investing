# Rebound Capital framework — condensed rules

## Philosophy
- Target ~15% CAGR (2x every ~5y). The aim isn't to beat the index every year. Be opportunistic when the market gets less efficient in the short term.
- Risk = probability of a **permanent loss of capital**, not volatility. If the business is intact, risk *falls* as the price falls.
- The key distinction: uncertainty (sentiment, narrative) vs risk (the fundamentals actually weakening).
- Buy: quality companies · FCF growing ≥~10% CAGR · price >20% below IV · clear catalysts.

## Idea sources
Sharp drops after earnings (20–40% in a day; be twice as sure on valuation when the company missed its own guide) · sector selling off on a narrative (SaaS on AI fears, 2026) · 13F filings (40+ investors) · screens (weekly) · pitches on Substack and X · special situations (spin-offs, IPOs, holdco discounts, court cases).

## QGV
| Factor | Metric | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| Quality | ROIC | >20%, consistent |
| Quality | Gross margin / operating margin vs industry | top of the industry |
| Quality | GP and OP growth vs revenue growth | faster than revenue = widening moat |
| Quality | CFO / EBIT | >70% |
| Growth | expected revenue growth, 3–5y | >7% |
| Growth | FCF growth | ~10%+ |
| Value | PEG (P/E ÷ EPS growth) | ≤2 |
| Value | DCF | price < IV |
| Value | comps (EV/EBIT, EV/Sales vs peers) | cheaper than peers with similar or better fundamentals |
These are guides, not hard rules. The ratios can change fast, and there's always some judgment involved.

## Filter questions
1. Is it a quality company with low risk of a permanent loss?  2. Is it down on a narrative while the KPIs still look good?  3. Is the price fair or cheap?  4. Is the drawdown temporary?

## Valuation
- DCF (sometimes sum of the parts). Exit multiple on final-year FCF of **20–30x**. That's deliberately conservative, so you don't get stuck in a stock whose business does well while the multiple derates.
- Base = the thesis. Bear = misses its own medium-term guide or runs below the 3–5y trend (no crash scenarios). Bull = "dream the dream".
- Ask what the current price is discounting: between base and bear, or worse than bear? Compare with Street estimates; the gap is the opportunity.
- Also find the price that gives a ~15% CAGR. Buy near it.
- Reward:risk ≥ 2:1. Reward = base (or bull) − price. Risk = price − bear.

## Catalysts
- In the short run, stocks move on KPI beats and misses vs expectations (hedge-fund pods de-gross on misses regardless of valuation). A series of short terms makes the long term.
- Every name has its top KPIs. Buy good businesses that are down on temporary hiccups or a narrative the data doesn't support, while the KPIs still look good.
- **Turnarounds: wait until the KPI turns.**
- Catalysts also tell you when to sell: once the gap to IV closes and no further catalyst is left, sell.
- No lazy longs. MSFT's EPS rose 3.6x from 1999 to 2013, and the stock went nowhere.

## Rating and sizing
- BUY = below IV + meets the return hurdle + confident in the model + a credible path to closing the gap. Most names end in HOLD or AVOID. The BUY list should feel uncomfortable.
- Size by quality, how bad the bear case is, and how clear the catalysts are. Start small; add in tranches over 2–3 months.
- Limits: ~15% per stock · ~35% for the top 3 · ~30% per theme · always hold some cash.

## After buying
- Read earnings by starting with the KPIs vs prior quarters. Monthly or quarterly, review each holding from scratch (which scenario is playing out? has the moat changed?) and decide add / hold / trim / sell.
- At the portfolio level, don't get overexposed to one risk or theme.
- News: did it change what the business is worth? Most news only explains why the stock moved today. Ignore narratives. Macro matters only where it makes some companies more attractive than others (e.g. high rates favor high-FCF names). Macro can't be the bear thesis, since it hits every company.

## Lessons from their mistakes
- **CSU:** being early feels like being wrong. Tranche in and wait for the drawdown to mature.
- **LVMH / Novo:** a turnaround needs proof. Wait for the KPI. Exit when the KPIs don't improve (Novo: −10%, out in time).
- **ASML:** management guidance lags. Listen to upstream suppliers and customers when judging the bull case.
